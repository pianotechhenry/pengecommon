# For the sensitivity consultant

Romani words and rows marked 'for the consultant'. Statuses record what a dictionary says; none of these is cleared for any period.

**beng** (VERIFIED, 1593 (Vulcanius, as Beinck))  
Romani beng, the Devil, is inherited from Sanskrit vyaṅga, spotted or frog. Wiktionary records it in Vulcanius's 1593 word list, a continental source, and English and Welsh Romani both use it for the Devil. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/beng#Romani)

**chavo** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary's main entry, ćhavo, means specifically a Romani boy or son, from Prakrit chāva, a young animal, from Sanskrit. The 17th-century attestation in English Romani is not tested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%C4%87havo#Romani)

**cosh** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary calls it probably from Romani košter, a stick. It gives no date, so the 19th-century dating needs checking. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cosh#English)

**daj** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary has daj, mother, in Romani and Welsh Romani, comparing Hindi dādī and dāī. It does not give Sanskrit dhāya, and it does not date the word in English Romani. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/daj#Romani)

**drabarni** (FALSE)  
Wiktionary glosses Romani drabarni as a witch or sorceress, from drabarel, to bewitch, and traces it to Sanskrit darbha, sacred grass, not to drab, medicine. The healer sense may still hold in English Romani; ask before using it for Jem's mother. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/drabarni#Romani)

**drom** (VERIFIED)  
Romani drom, way or road, is a loan from Medieval Greek dromos, not an Indic word. Dates in English Romani are not tested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/drom#Romani)

**dukkeripen** (UNSUPPORTED)  
Wiktionary has dukkeripen as a fortune told by a fortune-teller, with no etymology and no date. Nothing supports a 17th-century attestation. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/dukkeripen#English)

**gorjer** (VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary treats gorger, Gorgio and gadjo as forms of one Romani word for a non-Romani person, and glosses Gorgio as a Romani term for a house-dweller. Dates are not tested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Gorgio#English)

**gypsy** (VERIFIED)  
A clipping of Gyptian, from Egyptian, because the Roma were wrongly thought to come from Egypt when they reached England in the 16th century. Spanish gitano has the same origin. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Gypsy#English)

**kushti** (UNSUPPORTED)  
Wiktionary gives cushty (spelled kushti too) as from Romani kushto or kushti, good. Its earliest quotation is from 1937, and nothing on the page supports a 17th-century date; the project's own correction says no record before the 19th century. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/cushty#English)

**meripen** (UNVERIFIABLE)  
No Wiktionary entry under meripen or meriben, so the Sanskrit derivation and the date are untested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/meripen)

**mochadi** (UNVERIFIABLE)  
No Wiktionary entry under mochadi, mokhadi, mochardi or mokadi. Okely remains the source; this is a word to take to the consultant, not to a dictionary. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/mochadi)

**pal** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
From Angloromani pal, brother or friend, from Romani phral, brother, from Sanskrit; English brother is a distant cousin. Wiktionary gives no date, so the 1680s needs checking. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pal#English)

**pani** (VERIFIED)  
Romani pani, water, inherited from Prakrit and ultimately Sanskrit pānīya. Wiktionary also lists it in Angloromani, Welsh Romani and even Scots. Dates in English Romani are not tested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/pani#Romani)

**ruk** (VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary has Romani rukh, tree, from Prakrit rukkha, from Sanskrit rukṣa or vṛkṣa. Dates in English Romani are not tested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rukh#Romani)

**vardo** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
Angloromani vardo, from Romani vordon, a borrowing from Iranian, from a root meaning to turn. The mid-19th-century date for the wagon is not tested here. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/vardo#English)

**vesh** (FALSE)  
Wiktionary gives Romani veś, forest, as a borrowing from Iranian (compare Persian bêše), not from Sanskrit vṛkṣa. Vesh is its anglicised spelling. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ve%C5%9B#Romani)

**veshengro** (UNVERIFIABLE)  
No Wiktionary entry. The parts are attested: veś, forest, and the agent suffix -engro, but the compound and its date are untested. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/veshengro)

**yag** (PARTLY VERIFIED)  
Wiktionary lists the Romani word as jag, fire, inherited from a Prakrit form aggi. It does not test the Sanskrit agni step or when the word is first recorded in English Romani. For the consultant.  
[source](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/jag#Romani)
